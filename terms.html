Well data: public records, not a survey

Well names, numbers, operators, statuses and locations come from the public records of each state's oil and gas regulator, as filed by operators, and are republished by Leaseway as they are. They can be incomplete, out of date or wrong: a location can be off, a new well can be missing for days, a status can lag, and a well with no location in the state's files can't be shown. Distances and directions are straight lines unless your maps app says otherwise, and GPS and compass readings can be wrong. Leaseway is a finding aid. It is not a survey, a legal description, a title record, or a substitute for the operator's directions, lease signs and site instructions.

Safety and access

Don't use your phone while driving: set the route before you go and follow the law where you drive. Leaseway doesn't tell you whether you may enter a property, lease road or location. Get the operator's and landowner's permission, stay on lease roads, close gates behind you, and don't cross fences or pipeline rights-of-way. Oil and gas sites can be dangerous (hydrogen sulfide, pressure, traffic, heavy equipment): follow the operator's safety rules, posted warnings and your employer's procedures, and don't enter a site you aren't trained and authorized to enter.
