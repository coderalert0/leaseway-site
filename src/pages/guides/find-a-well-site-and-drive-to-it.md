## What you were given

| You have | It finds | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| API number | One surface location | Texas often shows 8 digits, without the leading 42 |
| Lease and well number | Often several wells | The same lease name in other counties; "1H" on many leases |
| Operator and well name | Often several wells | Operators change; the state's record may be a step behind |
| Section, township, range | A square mile | Texas uses abstracts and surveys instead |
| Coordinates | One spot | Datum (NAD27 or NAD83), and surface or bottom hole |

## Before you leave signal

1. **Check the county and operator** against the work order. A right name in the wrong county is the usual wrong turn.
2. **Look at the pad, not only the well.** Several wells can share one pad, and the sign at the gate may name another one.
3. **Find the way in on imagery.** Map apps know county roads; many lease roads aren't on them, and a straight-line route can end at a locked gate or a fence.
4. **Navigate to where the lease road leaves the county road**, then to the pad.
5. **Save the maps offline**, and screenshot the directions anyway.
6. **Ask for the gate code and the H2S status** before you go, not at the gate.

## The last mile

Past the end of the mapped road, use the bearing and distance to the surface location and keep to roads you can see. If the arrow points across a pasture, look for the road that goes around. Record your track in: it is the directions for the next person.

Follow posted speed limits and signs, sign in where the site asks, and don't enter a location you aren't cleared for.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I find an oil well by its lease name?

Search the name with the well number, then match the county and operator. Names repeat, so confirm with the API number before you drive.

### Why do directions stop before the well?

Map apps route on roads they know. Private lease roads are often missing, so they stop at the nearest mapped road.

### Does the API number tell me where the well is?

It tells you the state and county. The location itself is in the regulator's record for that number.
